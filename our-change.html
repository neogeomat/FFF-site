Change led by FFFPO action

When producers organize, territories change

FFF invests directly in forest and farm producer organizations — financing, business support and peer learning, not top-down projects. Producers decide, producers lead.

Country highlight

Advocacy and policy reforms

FECOFUN advocacy

FECOFUN advocacy was central to securing provisions in the Forest Act 2019 that recognise agroforestry in community forests and allow CFUGs to establish enterprise provisions such as NTFP processing, timber businesses, and eco-tourism. FECOFUN has facilitated simplifying enterprise registration — the current provision allowing registration at the Division Forest Office. At the same time, FECOFUN advocated for amending provisions on multiple taxation on CFUG commodities, prompting Nepal's Constitutional Court to issue a stay order in 2024 on the Provincial Forest Acts of Bagmati, Lumbini, Karnali, and Sudurpaschim, creating space for policy reform and fairer community forest tax reform policy.

FFFPOs impacting national strategies

FECOFUN's lobbying led to the dissolution of the Scientific Forest Management procedure in 2021, which was replaced by Sustainable Forest Management. It also created an enabling process for the registration of small forest-based enterprises. FECOFUN advocated for tenure rights of CFUGs and inclusion of gender and equity provisions in local and national forest laws, and collaborated with the Ministry of Forests and Environment to address policy gaps in the Forest Guidelines 2022.

Outcomes include

  • Mandatory 50% participation of women in CFUG executive committees
  • Compulsory inclusion of women in leadership positions
  • Extension of forest timber harvesting periods for user groups

Impact overview

Results led by FFFPO action

Cross-sectoral

Multistakeholder mechanisms

FFF supported FECOFUN to facilitate 37 multi-stakeholder platforms, connecting producers with government, technical agencies, businesses and financial institutions.

Platforms mobilized training, registration support, raw materials, market access and financial linkages to address enterprise needs. Dialogue amplified producers' voices and informed engagement on local forest legislation, PGS guidelines and enterprise barriers.

Continued effectiveness requires institutional ownership, clear responsibilities, follow-up and resources.

Markets

Entrepreneurship and financial linkages

FFF strengthens forest and farm producer organizations as hubs for entrepreneurship, business services, market access and financial linkages.

More than 20 enterprises have established or expanded processing and value addition, while 14 women-led enterprises have received support across diverse sectors.

These investments connect sustainable resource management with local economic opportunities, although emerging enterprises continue to need tailored technical, institutional and financial support.

Climate

Resilience, landscapes and livelihoods

By 2026, supported FFFPOs managed approximately 73,000 hectares, with 3,344 hectares directly supported through protection, restoration and sustainable management.

34 CFUGs developed climate resilience plans covering 34,115 hectares, while approximately 36,016 people benefited from improved incomes or more sustainable livelihoods, including women and marginalized communities.

Social

Social and cultural services

FFF supports FFFPOs to strengthen community resilience through social welfare, health services, employment, and cultural and traditional knowledge and skills.

Examples include Binayi CFUG's NPR 250,000 Social Welfare Fund, community health camps, women's employment, and enterprises sustaining Chepang and Tharu traditions.

These initiatives strengthen social cohesion and inclusion, while requiring closer links with public services and better documentation of results.

Policy

FFFPOs influencing policy

FECOFUN secured recognition of agroforestry and community forest enterprises in the Forest Act 2019, simplified enterprise registration, and challenged multiple taxation on CFUG products — creating space for fairer taxation and a more enabling environment for community businesses.

NFGF's advocacy supported local agricultural legislation and Participatory Guarantee System (PGS) guidelines, translating farmers' priorities into local policy. These efforts strengthened institutional support for sustainable farming, farmer-led quality assurance and market access.

Direct investment, not assistance

Small grants (USD 10–50k) go straight to organizations. Business plans, financial controls and accountability are built together, then funds are managed by the organization itself.

Why it works

Organizations are closest to the forest. When they have secure rights, services and markets, restoration and production reinforce each other.

Our Purpose

Stronger organizations → sustainable landscapes → resilient livelihoods. Forests managed by communities store carbon, feed families and create jobs.
